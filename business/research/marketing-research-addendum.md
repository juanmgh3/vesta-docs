## How to read this file

Label standard — **identical to `market-dossier.md`** (from the brief's number-verification section):

- **FACT** — directly stated by a credible source. A load-bearing figure only earns
  FACT with a **second independent source**; single-source figures are marked
  `FACT (1 source)` and are provisional until corroborated.
- **ESTIMATE** — derived. The derivation and a source per input are shown.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible but unverified; needs founder validation or a real test.
- **ANALYSIS** — a judgement of mine (the synthesis), built on sourced observations
  below it. Kept visually separate from the facts it rests on, per founder protocol.

Adjudication rules applied throughout, stricter than the raw subagent labels:

1. **Two outlets reprinting one primary study are ONE source, not two.** Cetelem via Forbes +
   elEconomista, or a barómetro via two trade sites, is `FACT (1 source)` — corroboration of
   *reporting*, not of *measurement*.
2. **A study that exists and says what is claimed is FACT about the study**; its *transfer* to
   the Spanish deep-hygiene niche is HYPOTHESIS/ANALYSIS when the study is foreign or off-sector
   (most academic findings here are US or Italian).
3. **Snippet-only figures that a direct fetch could not confirm (403/404) do not graduate to
   FACT** regardless of how often they are repeated.
4. **Contaminated / obsolete / off-market sources are discarded and named** (see Gaps §).

## R1 — Consumer psychology of home hygiene

The dossier already grounds demand epidemiologically (respiratory allergy ~20%, SEPAR;
dust-mite sensitization >55% among allergic-respiratory patients in Com. Valenciana). R1 adds
the **psychological mechanism** layer and two demand nuances.

- **Under-diagnosis of dust-mite allergy [ESTIMATE — single source, commercial bias].** A
  Stallergenes European survey (n=4,016 adults; DE/FR/IT/ES; pub. 2012-04-12) reports **~60% of
  Spaniards show dust-mite-allergy symptoms but only ~15% of the symptomatic have a formal
  diagnosis** [S1]. The sponsor manufactures immunotherapy → interest bias toward "more
  diagnosis"; the 60% is symptom self-report, not clinical sensitization. **Do not use 60% as a
  load-bearing figure.** The *directional* claim it supports — allergy is prevalent **and
  under-diagnosed** — is independently corroborated by the SEAIC / Fundación BBVA clinical
  chapter ("in some regions dust-mite sensitization affects >30% of the population and 90% of
  asthmatic patients") [S2]. So **"dust-mite allergy is highly prevalent in Spain and
  under-diagnosed" = FACT (2 independent sources, qualitative)**; the two numeric figures
  (60% symptom-report vs >30% sensitization) use different methodologies and **must not be
  merged** — cite each to its own source. Primary Alergológica 2015 PDF not accessed [S3].

- **The "100,000–10 million mites per mattress" hook is NOT a verifiable fact [HYPOTHESIS —
  discard as a Vesta claim].** Omnipresent in sector copy, no primary scientific citation
  located [R1 gap]. This matches the dossier's caution on uncited mite-count marketing
  (dossier §2.2). The report may describe that competitors *use* such numbers; Vesta must not
  assert one without a primary source.

- **Disgust as a double-edged purchase lever [FACT about the studies; HYPOTHESIS for transfer].**
  A validated Spanish disgust-sensitivity instrument (CSA) exists, with "hygiene" as a distinct
  dimension [R1]. Guido, Pino & Peluso (2018, *Journal of Consumer Marketing* 35(1):105–115)
  find contamination-disgust *reduces* purchase intention for "experience/trust" products [S4];
  Morales & Fitzsimons (2007, *JMR*) document "product contagion" — a disgusting item transfers
  offensive properties to what it touches [S5]. **Both are FACT as studies; both are Italian/US,
  not Spanish home-cleaning** → the operational read (a deep-hygiene service is a "trust" purchase,
  so messaging must **show the resolved/clean state, not only the dirty problem**, to avoid the
  provider inheriting the disgust) is **ANALYSIS**, useful for ch3/ch7, not a measured fact.

- **"Invisible enemy made visible" is a real, in-use mechanism [FACT — technology; HYPOTHESIS —
  conversion effect].** UV-C fluorescence genuinely makes allergen residue visible on mattresses
  and is marketed in the sector [S6]. Two real Spanish/international cases build their pitch on
  the mechanism: **UrbanCleaner** (invisible "invaders", AITEX lab certification, reframed as a
  recurring 6-month health need) [S7] and **Chem-Dry** (carbonated method, no soaps, results
  guarantee) [S8] — both also in the dossier's competitor set. **No study measures the
  conversion uplift of a visual demonstration** in home cleaning → the persuasive efficacy is
  HYPOTHESIS, a well-attested market pattern rather than a proven law.

- **Reassurance/control > technical elimination [FACT (1 source, qualitative) + ANALYSIS].**
  Aggregated Spanish customer-review language (Aladinos and peers) centres on emotional relief —
  "sleep easy again", "peace of mind" — not on mites-killed metrics the client cannot verify
  [S10]. This is coherent with Protection Motivation Theory (Rogers): effective protective
  messaging needs threat severity + vulnerability + response-efficacy + self-efficacy [S9] —
  a consolidated framework (FACT it exists), applied here as ANALYSIS.

- **No status/luxury motive in the Spanish at-home-cleaning discourse [FACT (1 source) +
  ANALYSIS — a steering caution].** Market sources frame home cleaning as **democratised**
  ("no longer a thing for the wealthy"), with general home-cleaning at **€10–20/h, mean
  €12–15/h** [S11]. The *absence* of an active status motive is an inference by absence (no
  source documents status as a live driver in this vertical). **Steer:** ground Vesta's premium
  on **health + relief + control**, not social distinction — the evidence supports the former,
  not the latter. (The €12–15/h figure is *general hourly cleaning*, not the deep-hygiene
  per-piece market the dossier prices — context only, not a Vesta price anchor.)

## R2 — Premium & trust cues for a 45–65 Spanish audience (paid mobile)

- **The 55–65+ cohort is economically heavy [FACT / FACT (1 source)].** 65+ = **20.7% of the
  Spanish population** at 2025-01-01 (INE) → ~10.2M people [ESTIMATE: 49,128,297 × 0.207] [S12].
  The 55–74 "senior generation" ≈ **7.5M, +12% over five years** [FACT (1 source), OBS] [S13].
  **>40% of family wealth sits in 55+-headed households** [FACT (1 source), BBVA citing INE]
  [S14]. These strengthen the dossier's affluent-45–65 targeting with a spending-power rationale.

- **Seniors out-spend the average [FACT (1 source) — one primary study, two reprints].** The
  Observatorio Cetelem reports 55+ spending **+14% vs the national average in 2024** [S15][S16]
  and **+13% in 2025** [S16]. Forbes and elEconomista both trace to the *same* Cetelem study →
  **one independent source, not two** (elEconomista direct-fetch returned 403; figures from
  snippet). Load-bearing-adjacent, not load-bearing for the model; use as supporting context.

- **Contact channel: offer an inbound human line, never force outbound calls or bots
  [FACT (legal) + HYPOTHESIS (preference)].** Google Ads **call assets** can convert better than
  the landing for service seekers [FACT, Google docs] [S17]. Boomer preference for phone/WhatsApp/
  human contact over forms and bots is consistent across agency sources but has no primary study
  → **HYPOTHESIS** [S18]. Hard legal constraint (**FACT**): since June 2023 Spain **prohibits
  unsolicited commercial calls without prior consent** (fines up to €2M; Lista Robinson; AEPD's
  first Robinson sanction, €5,000 to Adverbis Spain, resolved 2025) [S19]. **Operational read
  [ANALYSIS]:** publish a visible click-to-call number for *inbound* calls; do **not** build an
  outbound cold-call motion, and avoid a chatbot as the sole contact path.

- **Reviews: Spaniards rely on them heavily; older buyers may need *fewer* — unproven
  [FACT (qualitative, 2 independent) + HYPOTHESIS].** Two independent studies both show a large
  majority consult reviews before buying: **89%** (Skeepers, ~10k Spanish respondents, 2025)
  [S36][S37, R4] and **75%** (Barómetro eCommerce 2024) [S20][S21] → **the qualitative claim is
  FACT (2 independent studies)**; the two percentages are single-study each and must not be
  merged. The narrower claims that **45–65 buyers are the *least* review-conditioned and prefer
  small review volumes (<50, or ≤3 read)** could not be traced to a primary source → **HYPOTHESIS**
  [R2]. **Steer [ANALYSIS]:** a handful of credible, recent, verifiable reviews beats a wall of
  hundreds for this cohort — but present it as a working hypothesis, not a measured fact.

- **Word of mouth is the most-trusted channel [FACT (1 source, global)].** 88% trust
  recommendations from friends/family (Nielsen Global Trust in Advertising 2021, 40k respondents,
  56 countries) [S22] — global, not Spain-specific; directional. Complements the dossier's Spanish
  referral proxy (HomeServe, §6.4 / R4 below).

- **Trust seals / certification help, but curated [FACT (1 source) ×2].** ~67% of Spaniards know
  seals like Confianza Online and ~80% consider them important; ~23% would abandon a purchase
  without one [S23] (PDF not opened directly — snippet-confirmed, treat as FACT (1 source)). An
  IPSOS/CONFICERT study: **75% prefer certified products, 66% would pay more, 60% associate seals
  with quality**, but **64% want *fewer* seals with more oversight** [S24]. **Steer [ANALYSIS]:**
  one or two clearly-explained certifications (what they guarantee, by whom) beat a row of generic
  logos — directly reinforces the dossier's AITEX-style "verified proof is ownable" gap (§4.2).

- **Low financial literacy → authority/familiarity heuristics [FACT (1 source) + ANALYSIS].**
  Only 51% of Spaniards answer a basic risk-diversification question correctly (Funcas / Encuesta
  de Competencias Financieras) [S25]. Combined with scam-anxiety, this points to decisions made on
  **external authority + familiarity signals** (recognised brand, personal recommendation, known
  seal) rather than analytic attribute comparison — ANALYSIS, not a measured 45–65 heuristic.

- **Price framing for this cohort [HYPOTHESIS / NOT FOUND].** Anchoring ("was X, now Y") is a
  general behavioural-economics effect (Kahneman/Tversky lineage) but **no Spain-specific,
  age-segmented study** on price framing for 45–65 buyers was found [R2 gap]. The reasoned (not
  sourced) read: aggressive urgency/countdown tactics likely *underperform* with a scam-wary,
  clarity-seeking older buyer vs a single clear justified price — **HYPOTHESIS**, flag for test.

- **Premium-vs-cheap signalling [FACT (industry consensus, qualitative)].** Careful design
  (consistent type, quality imagery, clear navigation, zero errors) reads as credible; generic
  templates erode trust — industry consensus, not a quantified 45–65 study [R2]. Seniors show
  higher brand loyalty and weight personal treatment/quality above price (BBVA, qualitative)
  [S14]. Supports the spec's "premium web is itself a trust asset" thesis (ch3).

## R3 — The Vesta name & the classical/Renaissance archetype

- **Vesta mythology [FACT — mostly 2 independent language sources].** Roman **virgin goddess of
  the hearth, home and family**; Greek equivalent **Hestia**; her presence *was* the temple's
  sacred fire (*ignes aeternum*), whose extinction was read as a portent for the Roman State; the
  **Vestal** priestesses held a 30-year chastity vow and guarded the flame [S26][S27][S28]. Festival
  **Vestalia, 7–15 June**; the cult was among Rome's last non-Christian rites (defunded 382,
  closed 391, last Vestal 394 AD) — the later/date-specific points are `FACT (1 source)` [S26].
  **Precision caveat for the report [FACT]:** Vesta has **no narrative mythology** (no births,
  loves, deeds) — she is an *abstract function* deity (home, purity, protective fire, continuity).
  The name's brand value therefore rests on **meaning**, not on any anecdote to retell.

- **Cultural-capital transfer [FACT (theory) + HYPOTHESIS (application)].** Bourdieu's cultural
  capital explains the mechanism: cultured references act as symbolic currency signalling status,
  transferable by association (halo) to a brand that invokes them [S29]. FACT as established
  theory; its application to a cleaning brand invoking classical art is **HYPOTHESIS** — coherent,
  not empirically tested for this case.

- **Brand antiquity commands a price premium — with a Spanish-linked precedent [FACT — study
  exists and finding stands; off-sector].** Baumert & de Obesso (2021, *Journal of Business
  Research* 123:241–254) find **explicitly-signalled brand antiquity raises willingness to pay**
  (effect non-linear, moderated by familiarity) [S30][S31]. One author is Spanish-affiliated —
  a rare **academic** anchor for "heritage/permanence → premium" with a Spanish connection.
  **Not** Spain-market or cleaning-sector empirics → cite as the theoretical/academic basis for
  the premium argument, not as a Vesta-specific measurement. "Pattern interrupt" and "heritage
  cues" have industry/aggregate support but no single peer-reviewed anchor fetched → **HYPOTHESIS
  / FACT (1 source, aggregate)** respectively.

- **The Shopify "Renaissance Edition" precedent — with a factual correction [FACT].** Shopify's
  "Winter '26 Edition", promoted as the **Renaissance Edition**, launched **2025-12-10** with 150+
  AI features and an explicit Renaissance framing ("Tools don't create masterpieces. People do.",
  Michelangelo/da Vinci references) [S33][S34]. Its promo microsite **won Awwwards *Site of the
  Day* on 2026-02-09, score 7.92/10** [S32]. **CORRECTION carried forward:** the spec/tasks call
  this "Awwwards SOTM (Site of the Month) Feb 2026" — that could **not** be confirmed; the
  verified award is **Site of the *Day***. Use "Site of the Day (9 Feb 2026)" in the report unless
  a Site-of-the-Month listing is independently verified. It genuinely pairs generative
  Renaissance-style painting with modern-commerce UI (two-colour palette #F7F7EE/#292919) — a
  documented, on-point precedent for antique-meets-modern positioning.

- **contralabs.com — RESOLVED by founder (R3 subagent misread) [directional design-lineage
  reference].** The R3 subagent fetched the site's textual/SaaS surface and, unable to *perceive*
  design imagery via a text fetch, wrongly concluded "no classical imagery" [S35]. **Founder
  clarification (2026-07-07):** ContraLabs' design language DOES integrate antique/classical art
  with modern elements (e.g. laptops in many of their compositions); it is the stylistic influence
  the founder drew from for the antique-meets-modern device. **Use ContraLabs as a directional
  design-lineage reference (founder-stated influence), not as a fetch-verifiable metric.** Lead the
  report's precedent argument with the documented Shopify "Renaissance Edition" (Site of the Day,
  verifiable) and cite ContraLabs as an additional stylistic lineage. [Directional — founder
  observation; not a load-bearing claim.]

## R4 — Reviews & word-of-mouth in Spain for home services

- **Spaniards consult reviews before buying — reconciled [FACT (qualitative, 2 independent)].**
  **89%** (Skeepers, ~10k Spanish respondents, pub. 2025-03-04) [S36][S37] and **75%** (Barómetro
  eCommerce 2024, R2 §) [S20][S21] — two independent studies → the qualitative claim graduates;
  the numbers stay single-study each. US BrightLocal 2025 (71% won't consider <3★; 74% check ≥2
  review sites) is directional only, **not Spain** [S38].

- **Cronoshare lead economics — a new acquisition-cost anchor [FACT (model) + ESTIMATE (price)].**
  Model (FACT): consumer posts free; up to ~4 pros are notified and quote; pros pay per unlocked
  contact in virtual "cronos"; **no commission on the completed job** [S39][S40]. Cost per lead
  is dynamic ≈ **2–15% of the job's estimated value, ~6% average**; concrete example — a painter
  paid **€19.95 for 141 cronos**, one homeowner's number costing 60 cronos [S41][S42] → **ESTIMATE**
  (two consistent secondary reports, no official price list). This **enriches** the dossier's
  Cronoshare mention (which had only the €40–100 ticket): it gives a second, marketplace-based
  cost-per-lead reference alongside Google Ads. **Caveat [HYPOTHESIS]:** because pros pay per
  contact regardless of winning, the effective cost per *converted* client is materially above the
  6%-of-value headline.

- **Verified-review claims are a *legal* requirement in Spain, not just marketing [FACT — 2
  independent].** RDL 24/2021 (in force **2022-05-28**) makes it an unfair practice to (a) claim
  reviews come from real customers without reasonable verification steps, or (b) publish/commission
  fake reviews, and **obliges any business facilitating reviews to disclose whether/how it verifies
  reviewers** [S46][S47]. **Operational read [ANALYSIS]:** any "verified reviews" badge Vesta uses
  must be backed by a disclosed process (e.g. invite reviews only from confirmed completed jobs) —
  reinforces the dossier's "verified proof is ownable" gap while adding a compliance floor.

- **The "+1 star ≈ +5–9% revenue" figure — sourced, but US restaurants [FACT — 2 sources for the
  study; off-market].** Luca (HBS WP 12-016), corroborated by Yelp's own summary [S44][S45]. This
  is the **actual primary source** behind the dossier's §6.4 "+5–9%/star" — so it *confirms* that
  dossier figure and supplies its citation, but it remains a decade-old **US restaurant** elasticity;
  present as an illustrative order-of-magnitude, never as a Spain/home-services coefficient.

- **Referral dominance in Spain — dossier figure, precise citation [FACT (1 source), adjacent,
  dated].** HomeServe España (2018 fieldwork, pub. 2019-03-06): **40% find a home pro via
  family/friend recommendation, 35% via own trusted contacts, 25% search online**; post-hire
  priorities 35% speed / 26% professionalism / 21.3% guarantee / 18% price [S43]. This is the
  dossier's §6.4 word-of-mouth proxy with its source pinned — home *repair*, not cleaning, and
  dated; directional.

## R5 — Google Ads for a local premium home service in Spain

Net effect: R5 **corroborates and tightens** the dossier's CPC €1–3 ESTIMATE and CAC ≈€71, and
adds a Local Services Ads (LSA) channel plus a defensible launch-budget range. It does **not**
close the two weakest inputs (close-rate, exact niche CPC) — both remain unmeasured.

- **CPC €1–3 sits in the LOW band of Spanish home-service verticals [ESTIMATE — corroborates
  dossier §6.1].** Spanish neighbouring-sector CPCs (one agency source): plumbing/electric
  €2–6, locksmith €3–7, dental €2–8, **reforms/construction €1.50–5** [S51]; a dated (2018)
  "Home & garden" mean of **€1.87** [S57]. Cleaning is lower-competition/non-emergency → the
  dossier's €1–3 is coherent and probably optimistic for Madrid-capital generic terms.
  **Refinement [ESTIMATE]:** keep €1–3 as base but reserve **sensitivity to €4–5 for Madrid
  capital at peak**. US WordStream 2026 "Home & Home Improvement" $8.33 is off-market, ceiling
  only [S58].

- **Local Services Ads is live in Spain and pay-per-lead — a second channel [FACT + ESTIMATE].**
  LSA has run in Spain since Oct 2020 and is active in 2026 [S48][S49]; it bills **per lead**, not
  per click [S50][S51]. **Cost per lead ≈ €6–50, overlap band €10–17** [ESTIMATE, two agency
  sources] [S50][S51]. **Gap [NOT FOUND]:** LSA lists a generic "Servicios de limpieza" category
  but **no mattress/upholstery/anti-mite sub-category** — eligibility for Vesta's exact niche is
  unconfirmed and must be checked in the real sign-up flow [S49]. Google Guarantee verification is
  in-house in Europe (2020 context; 2026 continuity is HYPOTHESIS) [S48].

- **Campaign structure & levers [FACT (official) + HYPOTHESIS (staging heuristics)].** Three match
  types since 2021 (broad/phrase/exact); **broad is recommended only with Smart Bidding** [S52][S53].
  Negative keywords are standard [S54]; a DIY negative list (bicarbonato, vinagre, "cómo limpiar…
  en casa", tutorial, casero) is a reasoned **ESTIMATE**, not volume-measured [R5]. **Quality Score
  is a diagnostic, NOT an auction input** — the real levers are expected-CTR, ad relevance and
  landing-page experience evaluated per query [FACT, official — corrects a common oversimplification]
  [S55]. **Staging read for a solo operator [ESTIMATE]:** start on Search + phrase match with tight
  geo-targeting; move to broad + Smart Bidding only after ~30–50 conversions accumulate (industry
  heuristic, HYPOTHESIS).

- **Defensible launch budget for a solo operator [ESTIMATE — 1 agency source + official mechanics].**
  Recommended monthly Google Ads for a Spanish local business: **€150–300 micro / €300–600
  established / €600–1,500 competitive-or-multi-city** [S51]; daily-budget mechanics allow up to 2×
  a given day and ≈30.4× the daily average per month [FACT, official] [S56]. **Read:** €150–300/mo
  (≈€5–10/day) is the defensible entry to validate the channel before scaling — feeds ch9's
  proposed budget range. This is the first sourced budget anchor; label PROPOSAL/ESTIMATE in-report.

- **CAC ≈€71 is internally coherent, still not FACT [ESTIMATE — corroborates dossier §6.3].**
  Cost-per-lead for a well-optimised Spanish local service ≈ **€15–50** [S60] (consistent with LSA
  €10–17 and Search proxies). If ~€20–25/lead × a 35% close → ~€71 CAC — arithmetically consistent
  with the dossier, **but the lead→client close rate is still NOT FOUND for this niche in Spain**
  and is the funnel's weakest input. Spanish conversion-rate figures (Hogar/Reformas 7.33%) are
  **HYPOTHESIS** — the source admits they are US WordStream values relabelled for Spain [S60];
  they merely *agree in direction* with the dossier's 8% CVR ESTIMATE.

## R6 — B2B channel (hotels / short-term rentals / companies)

Net effect: R6 confirms the dossier's "B2B is real but under-quantified" and adds the **channel
map** and **regulatory frame**. The B2B spend figure remains **NOT FOUND**.

- **Who aggregates demand [FACT].** **CEHAT** (national hotel confederation): 51 member
  associations, **>16,000 establishments, 1.8M beds, 500,000 jobs** [S61][S62]; it runs a "Socios
  Colaboradores" sponsorship tier (members include Pikolin, a mattress maker) — but the join
  process/cost is **NOT FOUND** [S61]. Regional bodies for a Madrid+Valencia play: **AEHM**
  (Madrid, **330 hotels**, FACT (1 source, self-reported)) [S63] and **HOSBEC** (Valencia region)
  [S64]. **Coperama** purchasing central: **>1,000 establishments**, 30,000+ products, and it
  **intermediates services** (laundry, insurance, turnkey works), open to chains and independents
  [FACT, 2 independent] [S65][S66]. **Channel read [ANALYSIS]:** for chains, the efficient entry
  is the purchasing central, not the individual hotel; for independents and STR, it is the
  hotel director / property manager, not a central tender.

- **Regulatory frame — a real gating question [FACT + ESTIMATE-caveat].** If Vesta's anti-mite
  treatment is framed as a **biocide** application (TP18 acaricide), **RD 830/2010** applies:
  applicator certification/carné, technical lead, mandatory civil-liability insurance, only
  registered products, **ROESB** registration, 5-yearly ≥20h refresher [S67][S68]. **If Vesta uses
  only heat/steam/filtered vacuum (no chemical biocide), RD 830/2010 likely does not apply — but
  this was not explicitly verified for the "no-biocide" case; confirm with a legal adviser**
  [ESTIMATE/caveat]. **ISO 9001** is near-universal in Spanish cleaning tenders; **ISO 45001** is
  a weighted award criterion in public tenders (BOE examples: 4% and 11% weightings) [S70]. The
  circulated "**€122,000 threshold triggers ISO 45001**" figure was **discarded** — no primary
  confirmation [R6]. **ANECPLA** (pest-control association): 280 firms, ~75% of sector revenue
  [S69]. No **state** law fixes a mandatory mattress/upholstery cleaning frequency in hotels
  (only regional equipment rules, e.g. Andalucía's 18 cm mattress-thickness minimum for VUT —
  FACT (1 source, snippet), verify directly) [S71]; cleaning-frequency claims on commercial sites
  cite no legal text and are **discarded**.

- **How the service is contracted [ESTIMATE — single-company pattern].** One B2B provider
  (Host Dry Spain) offers three models — equipment sale / one-off outsourced service / recurring
  maintenance plan — and recommends professional mattress cleaning **≥2×/year**; no published
  prices [S72]. Not a confirmed sector standard. The observed pattern is that mattress/upholstery
  hygiene is **folded into housekeeping/maintenance**, not billed as a separate line.

- **Spend anchors — mostly absent [NOT FOUND / discarded].** No recent (2023–2026) Spain B2B spend
  figure for hotel/STR upholstery hygiene exists [R6 gap] — confirms the dossier's B2B NOT FOUND.
  A 2011 Madrid 68-room cleaning budget (~€6,084/mo cleaning) is **discarded as obsolete** [S73];
  the Mews per-room housekeeping $ figure is **discarded (not Spain)**. The only usable anchor is
  the B2C mattress price (~€60, range €30–100) with volume discounts **unquantified** [S74][S75].
  **STR entry [HYPOTHESIS]:** via the property-management company (which subcontracts cleaning),
  not the individual owner, for volume.

## R7 — Naming / tagline landscape of competitors (analysis, not copy)

Pure competitive analysis of the dossier's competitor set (direct observation of their sites).
No load-bearing numbers; feeds ch5/ch8 differentiation.

- **The category name-space is saturated with generic descriptors [FACT — direct observation].**
  ~11 of 13 profiled brands are literal "clean/limpi/tapi" variants with a modifier prefix
  (Superlimpia, Limptex, Clean-Home, EcoCleans, CleanNew, UrbanCleaner, Biomex, Lux Limpio;
  TAPICRIS, Tapinext/Tapinet) — interchangeable with each other and with the Google search term
  itself [S76–S85]. Only **Yaklean** (phonetic invention) and **Centauro** (mythological, but used
  with no semantic tie to cleaning) escape the pattern [S86].

- **Positioning axes occupied vs empty [FACT (observation) + HYPOTHESIS (the gap)].** Dominant
  taglines cluster on clinical health/hygiene (UrbanCleaner "por tu salud y tu bienestar",
  Superlimpia, Limptex) [S81][S77][S84]; only **CleanNew** claims "premium/exclusivity" outright,

## Gaps / NOT FOUND — and how to model them as ranges

Each unfound item below must appear in the report as **NOT FOUND** with the modelled range +
explicit assumptions, never as an invented point value.

1. **Lead→client close rate (Spain, solo premium home-hygiene).** No source anywhere (R5, dossier).
   **Model:** sweep **25–45%** (base 35%), stated as HYPOTHESIS; propagate through the funnel so
   CAC is shown as a **band (≈€36–111, dossier §6.3)**, not a point. Only first-campaign data
   settles it. This is the single most load-bearing unknown — flag it as such in ch6 and ch10.

2. **Exact niche CPC for "limpieza de sofás/colchones a domicilio" in Madrid/Valencia.** Only
   neighbouring-sector proxies (R5) + the dossier's Ubersuggest €2.18 point. **Model:** CPC
   **€1–3 base, sensitivity €4–5 Madrid-capital peak**, ESTIMATE; replace via Microsoft (Bing)
   Keyword Planner or the first live campaign.

3. **LSA eligibility for the exact niche.** Generic "Servicios de limpieza" exists; no
   mattress/upholstery sub-category confirmed (R5). **Model:** treat LSA as a *conditional*
   channel — cost-per-lead €10–17 base *if eligible*; verify in the real sign-up flow before
   committing budget.

4. **B2B upholstery-hygiene spend (hotels / STR / companies) in Spain.** No recent figure
   (R6, dossier). **Model:** do **not** put a € number on B2B; size it *only* from the B2C
   per-piece price (~€60, range €30–100) × plausible per-property piece counts, as an explicitly
   assumption-driven illustration, labelled HYPOTHESIS, "subject to viability".

5. **Spain-specific / sector-specific review-elasticity ("one extra star").** Only US Yelp
   restaurant data (R4). **Model:** use +5–9%/star as an *illustrative order of magnitude* with a
   visible "US restaurants, not Spain" caveat; do not treat as a Spanish coefficient.

6. **Whether 45–65 buyers need fewer reviews / are less review-conditioned.** Snippet-only,
   unverifiable (R2). **Model:** state as HYPOTHESIS ("a few credible recent reviews likely
   suffice for this cohort"); validate post-launch. Do not cite a percentage.

7. **Conversion-rate (landing) for this niche in Spain.** Only US-relabelled figures (R5).
   **Model:** CVR **6–10% band, base 8%** (dossier), ESTIMATE; measure on launch.

8. **contralabs.com as a Renaissance precedent — RESOLVED (founder, 2026-07-07).** The R3 fetch read
   the SaaS/text surface and could not perceive the design imagery, so it misread "no classical art".
   **Founder confirms** ContraLabs' design integrates antique art with modern devices (laptops) and is

9. **Confianza Online / Cetelem home-spend detail / Alergológica 2015 clinical figure.** Primary
   PDFs not opened (403/404 or download-only). **Model:** cite the snippet-level figures as
   `FACT (1 source)` / HYPOTHESIS as labelled above; do not upgrade without opening the primary.
